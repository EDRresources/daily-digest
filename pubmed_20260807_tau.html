1. Tau-induced mitochondrial reverse electron transport drives neurodegeneration

Neuron, 2026 · PMID: 42561934 · DOI: 10.1016/j.neuron.2026.07.012

Across flies, mice, and hiPSC-derived neurons, tau enters mitochondria and binds complex I subunit NDUFS3 to drive reverse electron transport (RET), raising ROS and lowering NAD/NADH; elevated RET in turn drives further tau hyperphosphorylation, forming a self-perpetuating loop that can be broken by RET inhibition. Senior author: Bingwei Lu.

Rationale: top-tier journal, first mechanistic account of a normal tau function (RET regulation) turning pathological, and a self-reinforcing tau/mitochondria loop that is druggable — directly relevant to tau biology and therapeutic target discovery.

2. Scalable human iPSC-neuron model producing endogenous seed-competent 4R tau

Science Advances, 2026 · PMID: 42536730 · DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aeg1445

CRISPR-engineered iPSC-derived neurons carrying FTD splice-shifting MAPT mutations (S305N, S305N/IVS10+3) express >75% 4R tau, develop hyperphosphorylated, mislocalized, seed-competent tau aggregates by day 28, and respond predictably to tau-clearance compounds in a HiBiT-luminescence screening format. Senior author: Karen E. Duff.

Rationale: top-tier journal and a genuinely useful new iPSC platform for 4R tauopathy drug screening — directly relevant to your lab's iPSC modeling and therapeutics pipeline.

3. Reproducible 3D human brain tissue model captures disease-associated microglia states

Nature Neuroscience, 2026 · PMID: 42552384 · DOI: 10.1038/s41593-026-02367-0

An iPSC-derived 3D cortical brain tissue model (neurons, astrocytes, microglia) sustains mature, viable microglia for over six months; when engineered for AD pathology it recapitulates amyloid deposition, elevated phospho-tau, and neuroinflammation, with microglia adopting disease-associated transcriptional states that anti-Aβ immunotherapy largely reverses. Senior author: Dominik Paquet.

Rationale: top-tier journal and a rare reproducible, long-lived microglia-containing 3D model — a strong candidate platform for TREM2/microglia mechanistic work and drug testing beyond standard 2D co-culture.

4. Proteomic signature of hippocampal neurofibrillary tangles across PART and AD stages

Acta Neuropathologica, 2026 · PMID: 42542447 · DOI: 10.1007/s00401-026-03064-9

Laser-capture microdissection of AT8+ tangles from 17 postmortem brains (PART, intermediate AD, advanced AD) and label-free DIA proteomics identified a 63-protein core tangle signature plus stage-specific enrichment: structural proteins predominate in PART, RNA-binding/translation proteins in Aβ-positive AD, with intermediate AD showing a transitional profile. Senior author: Thomas Wisniewski.

Rationale: top-tier neuropathology journal; direct human-tissue mechanistic insight into how amyloid co-pathology reshapes the tangle proteome, relevant to tau propagation biology and biomarker discovery.

5. p75 neurotrophin receptor drives Tau-mediated neurodegeneration via RhoA/ROCK

Molecular Psychiatry, 2026 · PMID: 42552421 · DOI: 10.1038/s41380-026-03810-1

In P301S tauopathy mice, p75NTR variants deficient in RhoA/ROCK signaling reduced insoluble tau, gliosis, neurodegeneration, and synapse loss, and improved spatial memory, with ROCK/GSK3β activity (the two kinases most implicated in tau phosphorylation) tracking p75-RhoA signaling status rather than neurotrophin binding. Senior author: Carlos F. Ibáñez.

Rationale: top-tier journal; identifies a new, druggable upstream node (p75-RhoA-ROCK-GSK3β) controlling tau phosphorylation in a well-validated mouse model — a plausible therapeutic angle beyond direct tau-targeting approaches.

6. High prevalence of tau pathology on PET in late-onset psychosis

Molecular Psychiatry, 2026 · PMID: 42547799 · DOI: 10.1038/s41380-026-03749-3

Using amyloid PET (¹¹C-PiB) and the broad-spectrum tau tracer ¹⁸F-florzolotau in 37 late-onset psychosis (LOP) patients versus 47 controls, 65% of LOP patients were tau-PET positive (versus 15% of controls), with elevated parietal tau signal persisting even among amyloid-negative patients and correlating with executive dysfunction. Senior author: Keisuke Takahata.

Rationale: top-tier journal; in vivo evidence that a substantial share of late-onset psychosis reflects underlying AD-like and non-AD tauopathy, a clinically underappreciated differential relevant to dementia diagnostics.

7. Sex-specific resistance to tau spread beyond the medial temporal lobe ("caTAUstrophe")

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42557961 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71716

In 872 amyloid-positive older adults across three cohorts, sex-specific penalized regression models predicting neocortical tau spread found that age and amyloid burden drove male resistance to tau spread, while clinical/cognitive and hippocampal-volume measures predicted resistance in both sexes — nearly all predictor weights differed significantly by sex. Senior author: Rachel F. Buckley.

Rationale: strong AD-focused journal; large multi-cohort dataset directly addressing sex differences in tau spread mechanisms, an area of genuine field interest with implications for trial stratification.

8. Astrocyte reactivity gates the effect of locus coeruleus dysfunction on tau and cognition

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42555262 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71703

In 78 asymptomatic adults, ultra-high-field locus coeruleus (LC) functional imaging combined with plasma GFAP and p-tau showed that lower LC activity/connectivity tracked with higher plasma p-tau specifically when GFAP (astrocyte reactivity) was elevated, and predicted cognitive decline only under the same conditions. Senior author: Heidi I. L. Jacobs.

Rationale: solid AD-focused journal; human in vivo evidence linking noradrenergic degeneration, astrocyte reactivity, and tau accumulation — connects LC pathology (an early AD feature) mechanistically to tau propagation.

9. Plasma p-tau217 trajectories track cognitive response to lecanemab

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42535277 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71705

In 153 early-AD patients on lecanemab, plasma p-tau217 fell significantly by 3 months (steepest decline 3–6 months, then plateau); patients with the greater biomarker decline had slower CDR-SB progression, while hypertension blunted the biomarker response. Senior author: Seong-Beom Koh.

Rationale: strong AD-focused journal and directly clinically actionable — supports plasma p-tau217 as an early pharmacodynamic readout for anti-amyloid immunotherapy response, relevant to trial design and monitoring.

10. CXCL10 drives female-specific tau pathology progression in tauopathy mice

Journal of Neuroinflammation, 2026 · PMID: 42557563 · DOI: 10.1186/s12974-026-03989-8

In P301S tau transgenic mice, disease-associated astrocytes upregulate CXCL10 within tau-pathology-rich regions; genetic Cxcl10 deletion selectively reduced tau accumulation and prolonged survival in female (but not male) mice at 9 months, despite reducing parenchymal T cells in both sexes — pointing to sex-dependent downstream mechanisms.

Rationale: solid neuroinflammation-focused journal; a concrete female-specific mouse mechanism (astrocyte-CXCL10 axis) for sex differences in tauopathy, complementing the human sex-difference findings above.

11. Chaperone DNAJB6b suppresses tau fibril formation by co-aggregation

Communications Chemistry, 2026 · PMID: 42547528 · DOI: 10.1038/s42004-026-02108-1

Using a tau fragment spanning the amyloidogenic core of ex vivo AD fibrils, the chaperone DNAJB6b was shown to delay tau aggregation by co-assembling with small aggregates and capping mature fibril ends, without binding tau monomers (confirmed by solution NMR) — reducing fibril mass and increasing tau solubility. Senior author: Sara Linse.

Rationale: mechanistically clean biophysical study identifying a specific, druggable chaperone-tau interaction mode; relevant to tau aggregation-inhibitor therapeutic strategies.

12. Immunoproteasome loss impairs microglial clearance and worsens tau and amyloid pathology

bioRxiv (preprint), 2026 · PMID: 42539361 · DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.14.738427

Crossing PS19 tau and APP/human-tau knock-in mice with immunoproteasome-deficient (L7M1) mice increased phospho-tau and amyloid-β pathology and microglial reactivity; microglia still engulfed tau aggregates but failed to degrade internalized cargo, with single-nucleus transcriptomics revealing a P2ry12/Trem2 microglial state with impaired phagolysosomal/mitochondrial programs, mirrored in human snRNA-seq data. Senior author: Natura Myeku.

Rationale: preprint, so unreviewed, but directly TREM2/microglia-relevant with a specific post-engulfment degradative checkpoint mechanism and human data cross-validation — worth tracking toward peer-reviewed publication.

13. PSEN1 E280A familial AD drives amyloid-independent, astrocyte-linked small vessel disease

bioRxiv (preprint), 2026 · PMID: 42538916 · DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.17.739026

PSEN1 E280A knock-in mice at 6 months show microvascular tortuosity, abnormal astrocytic end-feet, and ECM/RNA-associated proteomic dysregulation (GLUL, APOE, CLU) despite having neither Aβ plaques nor tau tangles, indicating that cerebral small vessel disease is an early, direct consequence of PSEN1 dysfunction rather than downstream of amyloid/tau; matching astrocyte signatures were seen in human familial and sporadic AD tissue. Senior author: Diego Sepulveda-Falla.

Rationale: preprint, but field-relevant genetics finding that reframes cSVD timing in familial AD and implicates astrocyte/BBB dysfunction as an upstream driver independent of the amyloid/tau cascade.

14. SPP1-mediated microglia-microglia signaling is AD-specific in human snRNA-seq

Glia, 2026 · PMID: 42552722 · DOI: 10.1002/glia.70210

Postmortem AD and control snRNA-seq cell-cell communication analysis found microglia-microglia SPP1-ITGAV/ITGB5 and GAS6-AXL signaling restricted to AD donors, arising from disease-associated and inflammatory microglial subtypes; RNAscope confirmed SPP1+ microglia enriched around amyloid plaques. Senior author: Bart J. L. Eggen.

Rationale: solid glial-biology journal and a specific, validated (RNAscope) microglia-microglia signaling axis in human AD tissue — a candidate mechanism for microglial phagocytic reprogramming relevant to TREM2-adjacent biology.
