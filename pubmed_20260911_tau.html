1. Plasma phosphorylated tau 217 concentrations, APOE genotype, and timing of cognitive impairment in individuals across diverse racial and ethnic groups: a pooled analysis of prospective cohort studies

Lancet Neurology, 2026 · PMID: 42716078 · DOI: 10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00313-3

In a pooled analysis of 8582 participants across seven multi-ethnic US, Canadian, and Dominican cohorts (16% Black, 37% Hispanic/other), higher plasma p-tau217 predicted cognitive impairment more strongly and earlier in APOE-ε4 carriers than non-carriers, with differences emerging 3–4 years before symptom onset. Senior author: Richard Mayeux.

Rationale: top-tier journal and directly relevant to dementia-disparities work — a genuinely multi-ethnic biomarker cohort with race/ethnicity-stratified data, informative for Deep South population applicability.

2. Large-scale single-molecule analysis of tau proteoforms

Nature Methods, 2026 · PMID: 42697987 · DOI: 10.1038/s41592-026-03188-6

A new single-molecule "Iterative Mapping" method using 12 site-specific antibodies resolved 130 distinct tau proteoform groups (up to six co-occurring phosphorylation sites) in organoids, mouse brain, and human AD tissue, revealing that phosphorylation events accumulate in an ordered, site-specific fashion rather than randomly. Senior author: Parag Mallick.

Rationale: field-shifting methodology in a top-tier journal — a new tool for resolving tau PTM heterogeneity at single-molecule resolution, directly applicable to your tau biology program.

3. Diagnostic performance of plasma pTau217 in genetically admixed South American populations

Nature Communications, 2026 · PMID: 42693124 · DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-76434-2

In Brazilian and Argentine memory-clinic cohorts, plasma pTau217 and pTau217/Aβ42 accurately tracked amyloid pathology and clinical diagnosis; African ancestry modified the APOE-ε4 effect on CSF but not plasma biomarkers, and body mass index, kidney function, and education independently shifted pTau217 levels. Senior author: Alfredo Ramírez.

Rationale: high field impact and disparities-relevant — demonstrates that ancestry and comorbidity confounders affect blood biomarker interpretation, a direct parallel to dementia-disparities biomarker validity concerns in the Deep South.

4. Modelling the temporal evolution of plasma p-tau217, amyloid PET, tau PET and cognition

Brain, 2026 · PMID: 41738322 · DOI: 10.1093/brain/awag075

Non-linear mixed-effects modeling in 2369 Mayo Clinic Study of Aging and 1591 ADNI participants shows plasma p-tau217 progression tracks cognitive decline timing as well as amyloid PET, with the expected amyloid→tau→cognition ordering breaking down after age 90, likely reflecting non-AD co-pathology. Senior author not confidently identifiable from metadata.

Rationale: top-tier journal, large dual-cohort validation of plasma p-tau217 as a progression marker with clear implications for trial enrollment and staging design.

5. Data-driven modelling of tau pathology reveals distinct progressive supranuclear palsy subtypes

Brain, 2026 · PMID: 41974128 · DOI: 10.1093/brain/awag131

SuStaIn modeling of 241 autopsy-confirmed PSP cases (Queen Square Brain Bank) identified three distinct spatiotemporal patterns of 4R-tau spreading, linked to MAPT haplotype, sex, and TDP-43 co-pathology, and generated novel regional tau-burden reference standards across MDS-defined PSP phenotypes. Senior author: Zane Jaunmuktane.

Rationale: top-tier journal, large well-phenotyped autopsy cohort — establishes network-based tau propagation heterogeneity in a primary tauopathy, relevant to tau spreading mechanisms broadly.

6. Fluid biomarkers in the evolving care landscape of Alzheimer's disease and related disorders

Lancet Neurology, 2026 · PMID: 42716045 · DOI: 10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00246-2

A field-level review argues that as p-tau217 and NfL enter routine clinical use, panels of fluid biomarkers spanning vascular, α-synuclein, TDP-43, and glial pathology will be needed to capture disease heterogeneity and copathology-driven treatment variability. Senior author: Clifford R. Jack.

Rationale: exception to the no-reviews rule — a field-defining consensus piece from the top neurology journal on the clinical trajectory of fluid biomarkers you work with directly.

7. Neuroimaging in the era of biologically defined Alzheimer's disease and disease-modifying therapy

Lancet Neurology, 2026 · PMID: 42716046 · DOI: 10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00285-1

Companion review to #6 outlining how amyloid PET, tau PET, and MRI now anchor eligibility, staging, and monitoring (including ARIA surveillance) for disease-modifying therapies, with AI and accelerated acquisition improving access. Senior author: Clifford R. Jack.

Rationale: exception to the no-reviews rule — companion field-defining piece, top-tier journal, direct clinical-trial and monitoring relevance.

8. Progress towards a biotypic biomarker profile for amyotrophic lateral sclerosis-frontotemporal spectrum disorders

Brain, 2026 · PMID: 42698276 · DOI: 10.1093/brain/awag297

Consensus report from a 2025 international workshop proposes "biotypic biomarkers" — TDP-43/tau-pathway markers agnostic to clinical phenotype — to detect ALS-FTD spectrum pathobiology before phenoconversion, and calls for multimodal panels combining these with genetic and cellular-aging markers. Senior author: David A. Wolk.

Rationale: top-tier journal, direct FTD relevance — a framework for presymptomatic biomarker detection applicable to FTLD-tau as well as TDP-43 spectrum disease.

9. Tau-associated neuronal loss in the intermediate nucleus of the human hypothalamus, a putative VLPO analog, in progressive supranuclear palsy and Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42712109 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71808

Stereology in 30 postmortem hypothalami found near-complete loss of intermediate-nucleus neurons in PSP (84% reduction) and progressive, phospho-tau-associated loss of galanin-positive (sleep-promoting) neurons across AD Braak stages, mechanistically linking regional tau burden to the distinct sleep phenotypes of each disease.

Rationale: high-quality neuropathology in a strong AD-specific journal — a concrete anatomical substrate connecting tau pathology to sleep disruption.

10. TREM2 as a central hub of neuroimmune-metabolic crosstalk in central nervous system disorders: from microglial biology to therapeutic targeting

Frontiers in Immunology, 2026 · PMID: 42694654 · DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2026.1912989

Review synthesizing TREM2-DAP12-SYK/PLCγ2/PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling across AD, PD, stroke, epilepsy, and ALS, explicitly flagging that TREM2's effects on tau-driven neurodegeneration (as opposed to amyloid/lipid handling) remain controversial, and arguing for stage-specific rather than uniformly agonistic or antagonistic targeting.

Rationale: direct hub relevance to your TREM2 program — useful synthesis of the open controversy around TREM2-tau crosstalk specifically, though a mid-tier-journal review rather than primary data.

11. Effect of tau silencing on tau pathology propagation, a treatment efficient at the earliest stages of the disease?

Neurobiology of Disease, 2026 · PMID: 42710551 · DOI: 10.1016/j.nbd.2026.107598

In WT-human-tau mice seeded with AD brain-derived pathological tau, Tau siRNA rescued spatial memory and reduced tau spreading only when started simultaneously with seeding; started after lesions were established, it had no benefit on cognition or pathology propagation. Senior author: Karelle Leroy.

Rationale: direct therapeutic-timing relevance for tau-lowering strategies (siRNA/ASO) — a cautionary, well-controlled mouse model result on the window for efficacy.

12. A third-generation, high-affinity biparatopic anti-tau antibody inhibits intracellular tau aggregation seeded by Alzheimer's brain extracts

Alzheimer's Research & Therapy, 2026 · PMID: 42693483 · DOI: 10.1186/s13195-026-02155-9

NIDB-3101, a sub-nanomolar-affinity biparatopic antibody targeting tau's microtubule-binding and C-terminal domains, captured a broad spectrum of pathological tau species from human AD brain extracts and outperformed benchmark antibodies in cellular seeding assays, with Fc-recycling mutations extending predicted half-life. Senior author: Jean-Philippe Courade.

Rationale: novel therapeutic modality addressing the high-dosing limitation of current tau immunotherapies, validated against human AD extracts rather than recombinant tau alone.

13. From target engagement to translational disconnect: A systematic review and narrative synthesis of direct tau-targeted clinical trials in Alzheimer's disease and primary tauopathies

Pharmacological Research, 2026 · PMID: 42702342 · DOI: 10.1016/j.phrs.2026.108439

Systematic review of 11 tau-targeted trials (monoclonal antibodies, vaccines, ASO, small-molecule aggregation inhibitors) finds consistent target engagement (e.g., BIIB080's CSF tau reduction) without reliable translation to cognitive/functional benefit, and argues the gap reflects disease-stage timing and tau heterogeneity rather than trial methodology. Senior author: Yun Lu.

Rationale: directly informs clinical-trial design and feasibility for tau-targeted therapeutics — a sober, well-conducted synthesis of why the field's tau trials have underperformed on target engagement alone.

14. Brain multiomic profiling identifies tau-related transcriptomic dysregulation in Alzheimer's disease

Brain Communications, 2026 · PMID: 42712681 · DOI: 10.1093/braincomms/fcag326

Transcriptome-wide association analysis of temporal cortex from 477 AD donors found most of 5740 Bonferroni-significant endophenotype associations were with brain tau levels; a high-total/low-phospho-tau state associated with synaptic and DNA-repair gene programs, while a low-total/high-phospho-tau state showed upregulated vascular/immune and downregulated mitochondrial/myelin programs. Senior author: Nilüfer Ertekin-Taner.

Rationale: large, well-powered multi-omic dataset directly tying transcriptional states to tau biochemistry — candidate pathway targets for follow-up in your mouse/iPSC systems.
