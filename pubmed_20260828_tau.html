Top-Ranked New Papers (15 of 125 shown)

1. Aberrant excitatory neuronal ERBB4 promotes Alzheimer's disease pathology

Nature, 2026 · PMID: 42649291 · DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10964-z

In AD mice, astrocytes and microglia selectively increase phagocytic elimination of excitatory (not inhibitory) synapses, and single-nucleus RNA-seq traces this to aberrant ERBB4 signaling in excitatory neurons that recruits glial engulfment independent of classical neuroinflammation. Senior author: Won-Suk Chung.

Rationale: top-tier journal and field-shifting — challenges the assumption that neuroinflammation alone drives early AD synapse loss, identifying a neuron-intrinsic signal instead.

2. Engineered autophagy receptors administered with extracellular vesicles eliminate pathological Tau and TDP-43

Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2026 · PMID: 42649384 · DOI: 10.1038/s41551-026-01774-9

Fusing the autophagosome-recruiting protein LC3A to cytoplasm-stable anti-Tau and anti-TDP-43 antibody fragments, then delivering the constructs via extracellular vesicles, cleared both pathological Tau and TDP-43 inclusions — a single platform addressing the FTD/ALS proteinopathy spectrum. Senior author: Derrick Gibbings.

Rationale: top-tier journal, direct hit on your program — a novel dual-target degrader modality for tau and TDP-43 with a plausible blood-brain-barrier delivery route.

3. Cholesterol dysregulation in APOE4 astrocytes promotes α-synuclein pathology in miBrains

Cell Stem Cell, 2026 · PMID: 42636814 · DOI: 10.1016/j.stem.2026.08.001

A new iPSC-derived multi-cell-type "miBrain" model (neurons, glia, myelin, cerebrovasculature) shows APOE4 astrocytes accumulate cholesterol and drive α-synuclein pathology, with the authors noting APOE4 similarly modulates tau and amyloid burden. Senior author: Joel W. Blanchard.

Rationale: top-tier journal and a directly relevant iPSC brain-model platform for studying APOE4-driven proteinopathy, applicable beyond synuclein to tau biology.

4. Challenges in Studying Synapses in Alzheimer's Disease

Annals of Neurology, 2026 · PMID: 42625110 · DOI: 10.1002/ana.78331

A single-author perspective arguing that ApoE4, free versus aggregated Aβ, phospho-tau, and neuroinflammation each have distinct and incompletely resolved effects on synapse loss, and that resolving which is primary is essential for rational AD therapeutics. Senior author: Thomas C. Südhof.

Rationale: exception to the no-reviews rule — a field-defining perspective from a Nobel laureate synapse biologist, likely to shape how the field frames synapse-loss mechanisms going forward.

5. Divergent tau pathology profiles induced by distinct alpha-synuclein aggregates in non-human primates

Brain, 2026 · PMID: 42631513 · DOI: 10.1093/brain/awag284

Intrastriatal injection of small versus large α-synuclein aggregates from Parkinson's disease brains into baboons produced significantly higher phospho-tau expression with the small-aggregate strain, showing strain-dependent cross-seeding of tau by synuclein in a primate brain. Senior author: Benjamin Dehay.

Rationale: high-impact journal and the first non-human primate evidence that α-synuclein strain identity determines tau co-pathology — directly relevant to cross-seeding mechanisms in mixed proteinopathies.

6. Endolysosomal inhibition uncouples tau uptake from intracellular seeding

Journal of Biological Chemistry, 2026 · PMID: 42648363 · DOI: 10.1016/j.jbc.2026.113498

A genome-scale CRISPR screen from the Diamond lab found that loss of specific v-ATPase components reduces tau uptake by cells yet paradoxically increases intracellular seeding, revising the prevailing model that endocytosis and lysosomal rupture are obligate steps for tau propagation. Senior author: Marc I. Diamond.

Rationale: mechanistic revision from a leading tau-propagation lab, with clear implications for which endolysosomal targets are actually druggable for blocking tau spread.

7. Disruption of sphingolipid metabolism promotes tau seeding through endolysosomal membrane rigidification and rupture

eLife, 2026 · PMID: 42657790 · DOI: 10.7554/eLife.106865

Silencing sphingolipid metabolism genes reduced endolysosomal membrane fluidity and increased vesicle rupture, and accumulated tau aggregates further worsened this membrane instability, identifying a lipid-driven feed-forward loop that promotes tau seeding. Senior author: Carmen Nussbaum-Krammer.

Rationale: novel, mechanistically clean identification of lipid metabolism as a modifiable determinant of endolysosomal integrity and tau seeding efficiency.

8. Presymptomatic plasma biomarkers in autosomal dominant Alzheimer's disease: sequence and timing

Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery & Psychiatry, 2026 · PMID: 42637536 · DOI: 10.1136/jnnp-2026-339037

Profiling 124 proteins across 270 longitudinal plasma samples from autosomal dominant AD mutation carriers and non-carriers defined the sequence and timing of plasma proteomic changes relative to estimated symptom onset. Senior author: Nick C. Fox.

Rationale: high-impact clinical journal, and directly useful for defining presymptomatic biomarker windows relevant to prevention-trial design.

9. Genetic frontotemporal degeneration across the lifespan? A critical appraisal of the neurodevelopmental hypothesis

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42659539 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71754

This review critically examines whether C9orf72, GRN, and MAPT pathogenic variants have neurodevelopmental effects that precede overt neurodegeneration, concluding that correlational human and preclinical data exist but no causal evidence yet supports the hypothesis. Senior author: Elizabeth C. Finger.

Rationale: directly relevant to your progranulin/FTD program — reframes how presymptomatic GRN and MAPT carriers should be studied and interpreted.

10. Tau-Targeted PROTACs Degrade Pathology-Associated Tau and Improve Memory in Alzheimer's Disease Models

ACS Chemical Biology, 2026 · PMID: 42631698 · DOI: 10.1021/acschembio.6c00096

Using methylene blue as the tau-recognition moiety, the authors built PROTAC candidates that induce proteasomal degradation of aggregation-prone tau and improve memory in AD models, with degradation efficiency tuned by linker length and E3 ligase selection. Senior author: Yue-Ming Li.

Rationale: a targeted-protein-degradation approach to an intrinsically disordered, traditionally "undruggable" target — a therapeutic modality worth tracking against your own tau-lowering strategies.

11. Targeting TTLL1 Alleviates Aβ-Induced Microtubule Disruption and TAU Pathology in Human iPSC-Derived Cortical Neurons

Pharmaceutics, 2026 · PMID: 42654155 · DOI: 10.3390/pharmaceutics18081038

In human iPSC-derived cortical neurons, the tubulin polyglutamylation enzyme TTLL1 contributes to Aβ-induced tau missorting into the somatodendritic compartment and microtubule destabilization; targeting TTLL1 mitigated both phenotypes. Senior author: Hans Zempel.

Rationale: relevant iPSC neuron model system and identifies a microtubule post-translational modification enzyme as an upstream target that acts before tau aggregation.

12. Tracking the Continuous Time Dynamics of Tau and Depressive Symptoms Across the Alzheimer's Disease Continuum

The Journal of Neuroscience, 2026 · PMID: 42637557 · DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2309-25.2026

Using longitudinal [18F]flortaucipir PET and depression-scale data from ADNI, three complementary time-series analyses support a probable causal link from tau accumulation to emerging depressive symptoms across the AD continuum, in participants who were not depressed at baseline.

Rationale: solid neuroscience journal and clinically useful — strengthens the case that depressive symptoms in aging can be an early behavioral readout of tau accumulation.

13. Investigating the role of EFhd2 protein in modulating tau pathology in a tauopathy mouse model

Frontiers in Molecular Neuroscience, 2026 · PMID: 42656731 · DOI: 10.3389/fnmol.2026.1833887

In a tauopathy mouse model, the tau-interacting protein EFhd2 modulates the balance between neurotoxic oligomeric tau species and comparatively inert neurofibrillary tangles. Senior author: Irving E. Vega.

Rationale: mid-tier journal but a clean mouse-model dissection of a specific tau-interacting protein's role in aggregate toxicity versus tangle formation.

14. The MR1/MAIT cell axis has limited effects on the start of tau pathology in a mouse model of tauopathy

Brain, Behavior, and Immunity, 2026 · PMID: 42628701 · DOI: 10.1016/j.bbi.2026.106969

Crossing PS19 tauopathy mice onto an MR1-knockout background (removing the MAIT cell-presenting molecule previously shown to affect amyloid pathology in 5XFAD mice) had little effect on early tau pathology or cognition. Senior author: Randy R. Brutkiewicz.

Rationale: a useful negative/limited result narrowing which innate immune axes matter for tau- versus amyloid-driven pathology, from a mouse tauopathy model.

15. Comparison of quantitative digital and semiquantitative assessments of tau pathology in relation to regional in vivo tau PET

Journal of Neuropathology & Experimental Neurology, 2026 · PMID: 42658568 · DOI: 10.1093/jnen/nlag093

In 52 end-of-life AVID A16 cases, digital image analysis and semiquantitative gradings of phospho-tau immunohistochemistry were strongly correlated across 19 brain regions and showed similarly robust associations with regional flortaucipir PET SUVRs. Senior author: Thomas G. Beach.

Rationale: a methods-validation study confirming that semiquantitative pathology gradings remain valid for clinicopathological correlation with tau PET, useful for anchoring interpretation of PET-pathology studies.
