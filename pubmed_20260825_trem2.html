1. Maternal sleep deprivation reshapes offspring hippocampal development through a Trem2-centered microglial program revealed by single-nucleus and spatial transcriptomics

Molecular Psychiatry, 2026 · PMID: 42629379 · DOI: 10.1038/s41380-026-03831-w

Using single-nucleus RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics across postnatal development in rats, this study shows late-pregnancy maternal sleep deprivation persistently downregulates Trem2 in offspring hippocampal microglia, disrupting synaptic refinement and LTP; AAV-mediated Trem2 restoration partially rescues the behavioral and electrophysiological deficits. Senior author: Zhifang Dong.

Rationale: top-tier journal, direct TREM2 mechanistic causality demonstrated via rescue experiment, and a genuinely novel developmental-origins angle on TREM2 biology relevant to microglial priming across the lifespan.

2. APOEε4 attenuates early TREM2-mediated microglial responses and influences disease trajectories in dementia with Lewy bodies

Research Square (preprint), 2026 · PMID: 42620051 · DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10429506/v1

In two independent DLB cohorts (n=129) with longitudinal biomarker follow-up, APOE ε4 carriers showed roughly two-fold lower soluble TREM2 during prodromal DLB independent of AD copathology, and higher baseline sTREM2 predicted slower cognitive decline. Senior author: Christian Haass.

Rationale: human genetics with functional/biomarker validation from a leading TREM2 lab (Haass), nominating an APOE-TREM2 axis relevant to disease heterogeneity and stage-specific biomarker strategy; flagged as a preprint, not yet peer-reviewed.

3. Immunopharmacological reprogramming of innate immune memory in Alzheimer's disease: From microglial priming to therapeutic resilience

Biochemical Pharmacology (review), 2026 · PMID: 42632547 · DOI: 10.1016/j.bcp.2026.118381

This review synthesizes epigenetic and immunometabolic drivers of maladaptive microglial priming in AD and proposes a biomarker-guided, patient-stratified framework combining NLRP3 inhibition, TREM2 agonism (citing clinical-stage agents VG-3927, MNA-001), metabolic modulators, and resolution pharmacology. Corresponding authors: Ahmed M. Abdelaziz and Reem M. Eladawy.

Rationale: a review, but useful for tracking the clinical-stage TREM2 agonist landscape and combination-therapy framing relevant to trial design; included despite being non-primary data.

4. Spatial multi-omics unveils sphingolipid metabolic reprogramming within the retinal pathological niche

iMeta, 2026 · PMID: 42630359 · DOI: 10.1002/imt2.70163

Combining spatial transcriptomics/metabolomics with knockout mice and microglia-specific siRNA knockdown in a retinal ischemia-reperfusion model, the authors show TREM2 directly binds SPTLC2 (rate-limiting sphingolipid biosynthesis enzyme), driving ceramide-centric metabolic reprogramming that amplifies inflammation; findings partly replicated in human glaucoma aqueous humor. Senior author: Honghua Yu.

Rationale: novel TREM2 protein-interaction mechanism (TREM2-SPTLC2) with direct genetic validation (knockout/knockdown), extending TREM2 lipid-sensing biology to a new CNS-adjacent tissue and a druggable node (SPT inhibition).

5. Phage Display-Derived Cyclic Peptides Target TREM2 and Modulate Microglial Responses Under Amyloid Stress

ACS Chemical Neuroscience, 2026 · PMID: 42615638 · DOI: 10.1021/acschemneuro.6c00304

Using a disulfide-constrained phage display library, the authors identify TREM2-binding cyclic peptides (TREM2-6, TREM2-12) with micromolar affinity that modulate microglial responses in human iPSC-derived amyloid-stress models and neuron-microglia cocultures, with supporting molecular dynamics and PK data. Senior author: Moustafa T. Gabr.

Rationale: a new therapeutic modality beyond antibody agonists, validated in an iPSC-derived microglia system — directly relevant to alternative TREM2-targeting strategies and model platforms.

6. Blue artificial light at night is associated with cognitive impairment involving alterations in microglial TREM2-TYROBP signaling and phospholipid metabolism: A multi-omics study

Ecotoxicology and Environmental Safety, 2026 · PMID: 42632171 · DOI: 10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120683

In a dose-dependent mouse model of blue artificial light at night, integrated transcriptomics/metabolomics link hippocampal cognitive impairment to dose-dependent downregulation of TREM2-TYROBP-SYK signaling and glycerophospholipid metabolic disruption. Corresponding authors: Tianyao Yang and Hui Hua.

Rationale: mid-tier journal and an environmental-exposure angle rather than genetic/AD-specific, but a novel modifiable-risk-factor link to the TREM2-TYROBP axis worth tracking for disparities-relevant environmental exposure work.
