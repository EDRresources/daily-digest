1. sAPPα acts as an endogenous ligand for TREM2 to regulate microglial survival and inflammatory responses in Alzheimer's disease

Neuroreport, 2026 · PMID: 42689388 · DOI: 10.1097/WNR.0000000000002307

Biochemical assays (solid-phase binding, pull-down, co-IP) show soluble APPα binds directly to the TREM2 51–71 region via its E1+Ac fragment, and that the AD-associated variants Y38C, R47H, R62H, and T66M reduce this binding. In LPS-stimulated BV-2 microglia, TREM2 knockdown worsened apoptosis and M1-like inflammatory polarization, which exogenous sAPPα reversed. Senior author: Ting Liu.

Rationale: high relevance — identifies a novel endogenous TREM2 ligand and directly tests how four disease variants (including R47H) alter that interaction, a mechanistic thread worth tracking against your own TREM2 variant work.

2. Soluble TREM2 Mediates Blood Brain Barrier Permeability through Astrocyte Reactivity

Research Square (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed), 2026 · PMID: 42687907 · DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10730833/v1

In an in vitro BBB model (primary human brain microvascular endothelial cells plus primary human astrocytes), sTREM2 drove a reactive astrocyte phenotype (AKT/NF-κB activation, GFAP upregulation), reduced Occludin at the endothelial tight junction, and increased barrier permeability, with astrocyte-derived MMP2 implicated as a mediator. Senior author: Joan W. Berman.

Rationale: high relevance and a genuinely novel mechanism — sTREM2 acting on astrocytes to disrupt the BBB rather than just signaling through microglia — but flag that this is an unreviewed preprint and should be treated as preliminary.

3. Stepwise Upregulation of Microglial Genes through the Slow Progression of Amyloid Pathology in Alzheimer's Disease Model Mice

Biological & Pharmaceutical Bulletin, 2026 · PMID: 42686621 · DOI: 10.1248/bpb.b26-00310

Using slowly-progressing App knock-in mice, microglial gene induction occurred in two discrete waves (12 and 18 months) that plateaued by 24 months despite continued Aβ accumulation, decoupling microglial activation from amyloid burden. Several canonical DAM genes were not induced, while three previously unrecognized genes (Ly86, Snx20, Pram1) were — and this induction persisted in Trem2 R47H knock-in mice, consistent with R47H having only a mild phenotype. Senior author: Nobuhisa Iwata.

Rationale: high relevance — a slow-progression AD model is a useful counterpoint to the aggressive-amyloid models typically used, and the novel gene set plus the R47H corroboration are directly on-topic for microglial DAM biology.

4. Persisting Region-Specific Microglia Activation and Synaptic Imbalance During Remission in a DSS-Induced Colitis Model

Glia, 2026 · PMID: 42683591 · DOI: 10.1002/glia.70208

In a chronic relapsing-remitting DSS colitis model, cortical microglia retained an iNOS-enriched activated state during remission (with a glutamatergic/GABAergic synaptic imbalance), while hippocampal microglia adopted an ARG1-dominant, TREM2/P2Y12R/F4/80-enriched reparative phenotype with region-specific (CA1 transient vs. CA3 persistent) morphological changes. Senior author: Ildiko R. Dunay.

Rationale: solid journal (Glia), and a clean demonstration that peripheral inflammatory remission does not restore brain immune homeostasis — relevant to gut-brain and TREM2-dependent reparative microglia states beyond AD per se.

5. Mitochondria-Endoplasmic Reticulum Contact Sites (MERCS/MAM): Multi-Layered Upstream Regulators of Microglial Senescence in Alzheimer's Disease

Ageing Research Reviews (review), 2026 · PMID: 42700866 · DOI: 10.1016/j.arr.2026.103352

Proposes mitochondria-ER contact site (MERCS) dysfunction as an upstream hub driving microglial senescence in AD via calcium overload, mitochondrial fission-fusion imbalance, lipid dysregulation, and ER stress, and explicitly addresses APOE/TREM2 crosstalk as bidirectional rather than strictly downstream. Applies a four-tier evidence-grading framework and notes >90% of current mechanistic evidence comes from non-microglial models. Senior author: Honglin Li.

Rationale: a review, but in a strong journal (Ageing Research Reviews) and unusually rigorous about grading evidence quality — useful as a critical synthesis of the microglial senescence/SASP literature and its (thin) direct evidence base, rather than another restatement of consensus.

6. The highest clinical trial failure rate for any disease in modern medicine: How both amyloid- and non-amyloid-targeting therapies failed to alter disease course in Alzheimer's disease

Free Neuropathology (perspective/review), 2026 · PMID: 42683436 · DOI: 10.17879/freeneuropathology-2026-9650

Classifies 497 Phase 2/3 AD trials (2010–2026) by mechanism using the CADRO ontology and argues the field's near-uniform failure across amyloid, tau, neuroinflammation (including TREM2 agonism), metabolic, and synaptic mechanisms reflects institutional and regulatory path-dependency as much as biology. Notes DIAN-TU prevention-trial data as the one strategy not yet refuted by the accumulated record. Senior author: Michael A. S. Guth.

Rationale: a review/perspective rather than primary data, and in a smaller journal, but directly relevant to your clinical trial portfolio — a pointed, well-referenced argument about trial design and Go/No-Go decision-making that's worth a read even if you disagree with its framing.

7. Mesenchymal stem cell secretome attenuates disease-associated microglial activation and cognitive decline in TBI-associated neuroinflammation

Neurochemistry International, 2026 · PMID: 42342036 · DOI: 10.1016/j.neuint.2026.106210

Intranasal adipose-stem-cell conditioned media given after controlled cortical impact TBI in mice reduced astrogliosis, apoptosis, and DAM gene induction (APOE, TYROBP, TREM2), and rescued learning/memory deficits at 7 and 30 days. Senior author: Rajashekhar Gangaraju (UAB).

Rationale: a UAB colleague's paper — DAM/TYROBP/TREM2 modulation by a non-invasive intranasal cell-secretome therapy in a standard TBI model, potentially relevant to shared interests in microglial DAM signaling as a druggable node.

8. Ursodeoxycholic acid inhibits platelet activation and thrombosis via TREM2: Evidence from mouse models and human studies

British Journal of Pharmacology, 2026 · PMID: 42386516 · DOI: 10.1111/bph.70567

UDCA binds platelet TREM2 directly (confirmed by docking and surface plasmon resonance) and acts as a TREM2 agonist, enhancing SHIP1 and suppressing Akt signaling to inhibit platelet activation; effects were lost in TREM2-deficient platelets, and UDCA showed antithrombotic efficacy with less bleeding risk than clopidogrel in mice and reduced troponin in ACS patients. Senior author: Zhongren Ding.

Rationale: not a neuro paper, but establishes UDCA as a validated small-molecule TREM2 agonist with a defined mechanism and human pharmacodynamic data — potentially relevant chemical-matter context if TREM2 agonism is ever revisited for AD despite recent clinical setbacks (see #6 above).

9. A polysaccharide extract alleviates neuropathology and cognitive deficits by modulating gut microbiota and neuroinflammation in an Alzheimer's disease model

Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2026 · PMID: 42676794 · DOI: 10.3389/fphar.2026.1830927

An orally-administered traditional-medicine polysaccharide extract improved spatial memory in 5xFAD mice, reduced Aβ plaque burden, restored synaptic proteins, upregulated TREM2, and reshaped gut microbiota; fecal microbiota transplant from treated donors reproduced the cognitive and anti-inflammatory benefits in recipient mice. Senior author: Weidong He.

Rationale: lower priority — a natural-compound/gut-microbiota AD study of a familiar type, included mainly for its clean FMT causality experiment linking microbiome shifts to TREM2 upregulation and cognitive outcome.
