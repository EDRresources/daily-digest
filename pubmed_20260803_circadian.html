1. Home-based tailored light therapy improves memory, circadian light exposure, and sleep in mild cognitive impairment

Journal of Applied Gerontology, 2026 · PMID: 42527889 · DOI: 10.1177/07334648261474211

In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of 61 people with MCI, 24 weeks of active home lighting (versus placebo light) improved ADAS-Cog memory scores, raised morning circadian-stimulus light exposure, and improved several sleep-continuity measures (senior author: Mariana G. Figueiro).

Rationale: highest relevance of the week — a positive randomized circadian-lighting intervention trial with a cognitive endpoint in MCI, directly bearing on circadian-based non-pharmacologic therapeutics for AD.

2. Overnight ventricular CSF sampling links hypocretin/lactate dynamics to amyloid-β42 surges, with only modest intrinsic circadian rhythmicity

Journal of Sleep Research, 2026 · PMID: 41607364 · DOI: 10.1111/jsr.70292

Hourly ventricular CSF sampling in 7 hydrocephalus patients showed amyloid-β42 rising overnight and surging after 8 AM, tracking hypocretin (orexin) and lactate more closely than sleep stage or endogenous circadian phase, suggesting wakefulness-linked processes rather than sleep or clock timing drive overnight Aβ42 variation (senior author: Marianne Juhler).

Rationale: novel human mechanistic data on orexin/wake-state control of Aβ dynamics, a druggable axis relevant to sleep-based AD therapeutics; small pilot (n=7) limits generalizability.

3. Data-driven sleep composites: quantity/efficiency and slow-oscillation-spindle coupling track cognition but not dementia risk

Neurology, 2026 · PMID: 42525904 · DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000218182

Across ~6,000 participants in the Sleep and Dementia Consortium, 9 data-derived sleep composites from 44 polysomnography/self-report metrics explained cognitive performance (notably sleep quantity/efficiency and slow-oscillation-spindle coupling) but none predicted incident dementia over 5-19 years of follow-up (senior author: Jayandra Jung Himali).

Rationale: large, well-powered multi-cohort study in a top clinical neurology journal; the null dementia-risk finding is itself an important negative result tempering enthusiasm for sleep-composite biomarkers, though not centered on circadian rhythm per se.
