1. Dendritic translation and neuroproteasome-mediated degradation of endogenous tau revealed by STARFISH

Nature Neuroscience, 2026 · PMID: 42595893 · DOI: 10.1038/s41593-026-02398-7

Using a new imaging method (STARFISH), the authors visualize local synthesis and neuroproteasome-mediated degradation of endogenous tau within dendrites, revealing that tightly controlled local turnover normally limits the misfolding-prone nascent protein — a control point that plausibly fails in Alzheimer's disease. Senior author: Kapil V. Ramachandran.

Rationale: top-tier journal, first direct visualization of endogenous tau's local synthesis/degradation dynamics — core mechanistic insight into how somatodendritic tau accumulation begins.

2. Different lysosomal insults, one microglial fate

Immunity, 2026 · PMID: 42580316 · DOI: 10.1016/j.immuni.2026.07.016

This preview discusses two companion studies (Balak et al. and Tejwani et al.) showing that genetically distinct lysosomal insults converge on a shared MITF/TFE-driven epigenetic program underlying disease-associated microglial states, linking lysosomal dysfunction to microglial transcriptional reprogramming. Senior author: Hui Zheng.

Rationale: top-tier journal; directly relevant to TREM2/lysosome-microglia biology — a convergent, druggable transcriptional node for disease-associated microglia.

3. Tau can wreak havoc in brain cells' energy factories

Science (News), 2026 · PMID: 42594210 · DOI: 10.1126/science.ael4194

A Science news feature summarizing new mitochondrial research suggesting tau disrupts neuronal energy metabolism, and proposing that targeting this tau-mitochondria axis could be a new Alzheimer's treatment strategy. No corresponding research author (news/journalism piece; not a peer-reviewed primary study).

Rationale: flagship-journal visibility of the tau-mitochondria axis, an area directly overlapping your own research — worth a quick read, though this is a News item rather than primary data.

4. APOE3 and APOE4 human astrocytes differentially modulate Alzheimer's disease pathology and microglial responses in chimeric mice

Cell Reports, 2026 · PMID: 42570239 · DOI: 10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117803

Transplanting isogenic APOE3- or APOE4-expressing iPSC-derived astrocyte progenitors into neonatal AD mice produced human astrocyte chimeras in which APOE genotype differentially shaped amyloid/tau pathology and microglial responses, isolating astrocyte-intrinsic APOE effects from neuronal or systemic ones. Senior author: Amaia M. Arranz.

Rationale: top-tier Cell-family journal; iPSC-chimeric model directly relevant to your lab's iPSC and mouse-model work on APOE/astrocyte contributions to AD pathology.

5. Plasma Biomarkers for Neocortical Tau Burden

JAMA Neurology, 2026 · PMID: 42573994 · DOI: 10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.2650

This study benchmarks plasma biomarkers (including but going beyond p-tau217) for identifying advanced neocortical neurofibrillary tangle burden, addressing the suboptimal performance of p-tau217 alone at high tau stages and informing patient stratification for anti-tau and anti-amyloid therapies. Senior author: Nicholas J. Ashton.

Rationale: top clinical-neurology journal; directly clinically actionable tau-staging biomarker work with immediate relevance to trial enrollment and monitoring.

6. Plasma P-tau217 for detecting amyloid clearance after donanemab in Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42565262 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71740

In a secondary analysis of the phase 3 TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 trial, plasma p-tau217 reliably tracked treatment-related amyloid clearance after donanemab, supporting its use as a low-burden biomarker for monitoring anti-amyloid immunotherapy response. Senior author: Oskar Hansson.

Rationale: top-tier AD journal and a major clinical-trial biomarker readout — directly relevant to real-world monitoring of approved anti-amyloid therapy.

7. A Small-Molecule DEPTAC Rescues Cognitive Deficits by Targeted Dephosphorylation of Pathological Tau

Advanced Science, 2026 · PMID: 42579441 · DOI: 10.1002/advs.77088

The authors engineer TP2, a small-molecule dephosphorylation-targeting chimera (DEPTAC) that tethers endogenous PP2A to tau, driving targeted removal of hyperphosphorylated tau in neurons and rescuing cognitive deficits upon systemic administration in tauopathy mice. Senior author: Jian-Zhi Wang.

Rationale: novel therapeutic modality (targeted dephosphorylation, PROTAC-like chemistry applied to phospho-tau) with in vivo efficacy — a genuinely new drug-discovery angle for tauopathies.

8. Selective Brain-Penetrant TTBK1 Inhibitors Modulate TDP-43 Pathology and Rescue Cognitive Deficits in a Mouse Model of TDP-43 Proteinopathy

Journal of Medicinal Chemistry, 2026 · PMID: 42593908 · DOI: 10.1021/acs.jmedchem.6c01882

New selective, brain-penetrant TTBK1 (tau tubulin kinase 1) inhibitors that spare the closely related TTBK2 isoform (avoiding ciliogenesis toxicity) reduced TDP-43 pathology and rescued cognitive deficits in a mouse model of TDP-43 proteinopathy. Senior author: Ana Martinez.

Rationale: TTBK1 sits directly at the tau-kinase/TDP-43 interface relevant to FTD biology; a selective brain-penetrant chemical probe/lead is a meaningful therapeutic-target advance.

9. Unique transcriptomic alterations in 5XFAD;PS19 mouse model identify glial lipid dysregulation and coordinated microglial-oligodendrocyte responses

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42576162 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71742

Sex-balanced single-nucleus RNA-seq of 5XFAD (amyloid), PS19 (tau), and combined 5XFAD;PS19 mice identifies transcriptional programs — including glial lipid metabolism dysregulation and coordinated microglial-oligodendrocyte responses — that emerge specifically under combined amyloid+tau pathology. Senior author: Jungsu Kim.

Rationale: top-tier AD journal; uses exactly the combined amyloid/tau mouse-model paradigm relevant to your group's model work, with a novel glial-lipid mechanism.

10. ABCA7-80 moderates vascular stiffness-p-tau217 association in older African Americans

Alzheimer's & Dementia (N Y), 2026 · PMID: 42582950 · DOI: 10.1002/trc2.70297

In a cohort of older African Americans, the ABCA7-80 (rs115550680) risk variant moderated the association between vascular stiffness (estimated pulse wave velocity) and plasma p-tau217, suggesting genetic risk shapes how vascular health translates into tau pathology in a population that bears disproportionate AD and vascular disease burden. Senior author: Mark A. Gluck.

Rationale: directly relevant to dementia disparities work — genetic-by-vascular interaction on tau biomarkers specifically in African American older adults.

11. Probing effect modification by socioeconomic status in associations between blood biomarkers of neuropathology and cognitive outcomes in the United States and India

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42581030 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71729

Using harmonized cognitive-aging cohorts from the US (HRS-HCAP) and India (LASI-DAD), the authors show socioeconomic status modifies the strength of association between blood neuropathology biomarkers and cognitive outcomes, with implications for biomarker interpretation in under-resourced and disparity-affected populations. Senior author: Eileen M. Crimmins.

Rationale: directly relevant to dementia-disparities research — cross-national evidence that biomarker-cognition relationships are not socioeconomically uniform, a caution for equitable biomarker deployment.

12. The UNC13A cryptic exon associates with cognitive impairment in Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's Research & Therapy, 2026 · PMID: 42596024 · DOI: 10.1186/s13195-026-02157-7

In a cohort of 1,672 AD cases, UNC13A cryptic-exon RNA levels (a TDP-43 splicing-repression target) predicted cognitive decline better than either the UNC13A risk SNP itself or the related cryptic target STMN2, and independent of TDP-43 pathology or survival. Senior author: Mercedes Prudencio.

Rationale: novel genetics/mechanism finding — pinpoints UNC13A cryptic splicing, not TDP-43 pathology per se, as a specific driver of cognitive decline in AD, a consensus-shifting nuance in the TDP-43/AD comorbidity literature.

13. Liquid-liquid phase-separated tau colocalizes with and stabilizes Aβ oligomers

Communications Chemistry, 2026 · PMID: 42575917 · DOI: 10.1038/s42004-026-02165-6

Biophysical experiments show that tau undergoing liquid-liquid phase separation colocalizes with and stabilizes off-pathway amyloid-beta oligomers, suggesting phase-separated tau condensates can act as a reservoir that sequesters and stabilizes toxic Aβ species. Senior author: Wolfgang Hoyer.

Rationale: mechanistically clean biophysics linking two core AD proteins via condensate biology — a fairly novel tau-Aβ crosstalk mechanism beyond simple co-aggregation.

14. Activated microglial phenotypes in the hippocampal CA2 subfield are implicated in Lewy body disease progression

Acta Neuropathologica, 2026 · PMID: 42570991 · DOI: 10.1007/s00401-026-03065-8

In autopsy-confirmed Lewy body disease cases with minimal age-related co-pathology, activated microglial phenotypes in the synaptically-connected hippocampal CA2 subfield tracked with the severity and progression of neuronal alpha-synuclein pathology. Senior author: David J. Irwin.

Rationale: top neuropathology journal; region-specific microglial activation as a driver of proteinopathy spread — a paradigm directly transferable to tau propagation questions in AD/FTD.

15. CSF fibrinogen predicts longitudinal Tau accumulation in cognitively unimpaired older adults

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42583778 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71720

In cognitively unimpaired older adults, baseline CSF fibrinogen (a marker of blood-brain barrier leakage) predicted the rate of longitudinal tau accumulation, linking early vascular/BBB dysfunction to subsequent tau progression before symptom onset. Senior author: Ricardo S. Osorio.

Rationale: top-tier AD journal; BBB dysfunction as an upstream predictor of tau accumulation in preclinical disease — relevant to early biomarker and intervention windows.
