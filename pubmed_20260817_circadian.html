1. Napping, Sleep Apnea, and Cognitive Function: Cross-sectional Results from the U.S. POINTER-zzz Sleep Ancillary Study

The Journals of Gerontology: Series A, 2026 · PMID: 42596580 · DOI: 10.1093/gerona/glag198

In 686 older adults from the U.S. POINTER-zzz Sleep Ancillary Study, greater napping duration was associated with reduced processing speed overall, and with worse executive function specifically among participants with sleep apnea from under-represented ethno-racial groups; sleep apnea did not significantly modify the napping-cognition association in the full sample (senior author: Laura D. Baker).

Rationale: only primary research hit of the week; a well-powered ancillary study of a national AD-prevention trial (US POINTER) linking a circadian/sleep-timing behavior to cognitive domains, with a subgroup signal in an under-represented ethno-racial group relevant to dementia disparities work, though findings are cross-sectional and exploratory.
