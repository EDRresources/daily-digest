1. Dendritic translation and neuroproteasome-mediated degradation of endogenous tau revealed by STARFISH

Nature Neuroscience, 2026 · PMID: 42595893 · DOI: 10.1038/s41593-026-02398-7

A new single-molecule imaging method (STARFISH) shows endogenous tau mRNA is translated almost exclusively in dendrites, and roughly a third of newly made tau is degraded there by a neuron-specific "neuroproteasome"; disrupting this local proteostasis step promotes somatodendritic tau accumulation. Senior author: Kapil V. Ramachandran.

Rationale: top-tier journal, direct hit on tau biology — a novel proteostasis mechanism governing where and how tau accumulates, upstream of aggregation.

2. [preprint] GRN loss reprograms amyloid-associated microglia and limits dense-core plaque-associated tau spreading

bioRxiv, 2026 · PMID: 42619689 · DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.22.740167

Single-nucleus RNA-seq and tau-seeding studies in 5xFAD x Grn-knockout mice show that progranulin loss remodels microglial states around amyloid plaques and selectively reduces plaque-associated tau spreading. Senior author: Celeste M. Karch.

Rationale: direct convergence of progranulin, microglial biology, and tau spreading in a mouse model — squarely in your research program, from a leading FTD/tau lab; preprint status is the main limiter.

3. PLCG2 downregulation impairs synaptic function and increases Alzheimer's disease hallmarks in neuronal cultures

Nature Genetics, 2026 · PMID: 42601455 · DOI: 10.1038/s41588-026-02709-5

A high-content screen plus rare loss-of-function variant analysis shows that reduced PLCG2 (an AD-risk gene acting downstream of TREM2 signaling) impairs synapses and increases amyloid/tau-phosphorylation hallmarks in primary and human neuronal cultures. Senior author: Jean-Charles Lambert.

Rationale: top-tier genetics journal, TREM2-pathway biology validated functionally in primary neurons — strong fit to your TREM2 program.

4. MAPT splicing modulators reduce 4R tau and rescue tauopathy phenotypes in human neurons and in a mouse model

Science Translational Medicine, 2026 · PMID: 42616837 · DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.ady6759

Small-molecule splicing modulators promoting MAPT exon 10 exclusion reduced 4R tau and hyperphosphorylated tau in FTD patient-derived neurons (P301L, S305N) and corrected the 3R/4R ratio in vivo in an N279K gene-replacement mouse model. Senior author: Elisabetta Morini.

Rationale: top-tier journal, therapeutic modality directly targeting tau splicing, validated in both human neurons and a mouse model. (Also surfaced in Thursday's FTD/progranulin digest; retained here given its direct tau relevance.)

5. Astrocytic 4R-tau pathology drives aquaporin-4 (AQP4) mislocalization, glymphatic dysfunction, and neurotransmitter dysregulation in mice

The Journal of Neuroscience, 2026 · PMID: 42618512 · DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2184-25.2026

In a new GFAP-driven astrocyte-specific 4R-tau transgenic mouse (modeling PSP), astrocytic tau pathology disrupts the dystrophin-associated complex, mislocalizes AQP4 from perivascular endfeet, impairs glymphatic CSF/ISF exchange, and lowers dopamine and acetylcholine, producing parkinsonian and cognitive deficits. Senior authors: Yukui Zhang and Xuejing Wang.

Rationale: solid neuroscience journal, novel mechanistic link between astrocytic 4R-tauopathy and glymphatic failure — a new angle relevant to PSP/4R-tauopathy biology and to glymphatic clearance as a therapeutic target.

6. [preprint] APOEε4 attenuates early TREM2-mediated microglial responses and influences disease trajectories in dementia with Lewy bodies

Research Square, 2026 · PMID: 42620051 · DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10429506/v1

Across two DLB cohorts, soluble TREM2 was roughly two-fold lower in APOEε4 carriers during prodromal disease (independent of AD co-pathology), and baseline sTREM2 predicted slower clinical decline. Senior author: Christian Haass.

Rationale: TREM2 x APOE interaction from a leading TREM2 biology lab, extending TREM2 biomarker relevance beyond AD into DLB; preprint status is the main limiter.

7. Optimized plasma p-tau217 and p-tau217/Aβ42 cutoffs enhance detection of pre-clinical Alzheimer's disease across diverse participants

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42619350 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71763

In a cohort including Black, Hispanic, and non-Hispanic white cognitively normal participants, cutoffs for plasma p-tau217 and p-tau217/Aβ42 optimized for pre-clinical (rather than symptomatic) disease substantially improved detection sensitivity across race/ethnicity groups. Senior author: Gregory S. Day.

Rationale: top-tier AD journal and directly relevant to your dementia-disparities work — biomarker performance and equitable cutoffs across diverse populations.

8. Disease-specific tau polymorphs are associated with unique protein networks across proteinopathies

The EMBO Journal, 2026 · PMID: 42613429 · DOI: 10.1038/s44318-026-00897-8

Interactome profiling of conformationally distinct tau polymorphs from AD, PSP, and DLB brain shows each engages disease-specific protein networks, and a machine-learning classifier can distinguish the diseases from a small set of interacting proteins. Senior author: Rakez Kayed.

Rationale: strong journal, novel tau-strain biology with direct implications for understanding tauopathy heterogeneity and strain-specific toxicity.

9. [preprint] Tau reduction counteracts transcriptomic and behavioral abnormalities in an Alzheimer amyloid model

bioRxiv, 2026 · PMID: 42620345 · DOI: 10.64898/2026.08.03.741071

In 5xFAD mice engineered with two, one, or zero endogenous Mapt alleles, reducing tau rescued behavioral, weight, synaptic, and transcriptomic abnormalities without changing amyloid burden — even in the absence of classical tau pathology. Senior author: Lennart Mucke.

Rationale: from a leading tau-reduction lab; a core mouse-model finding tying endogenous tau to amyloid-driven phenotypes independent of tangle formation, relevant to tau-lowering therapeutic strategies.

10. [preprint] Tau isoform imbalance and aggregation are pathological hallmarks of X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism

medRxiv, 2026 · PMID: 42619813 · DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.23.26358614

X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism (caused by a non-coding TAF1 retrotransposon insertion) shows tau isoform imbalance and 4R tau accumulation correlating with repeat length, plus elevated plasma p-tau181, GFAP, and NfL, positioning XDP as a genetic model of tauopathy. Senior author: Clotilde Lagier-Tourenne.

Rationale: human genetics with functional tau validation, offering a novel genetic entry point into tau proteostasis outside classical MAPT mutations.

11. A plasma-based protein signature combining NPTXR, ACHE, and p-tau217 predicts progression to symptomatic Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42598783 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71748

Using longitudinal Knight-ADRC data with ADNI replication, a plasma protein panel (NPTXR, ACHE, and related synaptic/cholinergic markers) combined with p-tau217 substantially improved risk stratification for progression from cognitively unimpaired to symptomatic AD versus p-tau217 alone (hazard ratio 11.15 vs. 4.08 for the highest-risk group). Senior authors: David M. Holtzman and Carlos Cruchaga.

Rationale: top-tier AD journal, from prominent labs, with direct relevance to clinically scalable p-tau217-based risk stratification.

12. [preprint] A toxic tau-PFKFB3 circuit reduces F2,6BP levels and drives neurodegeneration

bioRxiv, 2026 · PMID: 42619790 · DOI: 10.64898/2026.05.28.728580

Tau aggregates sequester PFKFB3, depleting fructose-2,6-bisphosphate (F2,6BP), which normally supports DNA repair and limits tau phosphorylation; F2,6BP supplementation rescued pathology across induced neurons, primary neurons, organotypic slices, and an AD mouse model. Senior author: Gourisankar Ghosh.

Rationale: novel tau-metabolism mechanism validated across primary neurons and a mouse model, with a clear therapeutic-target angle; preprint status is the main limiter.

13. NAT10 inhibition corrects nuclear defects in tau mutant human neurons and extends lifespan in a tauopathy model

iScience, 2026 · PMID: 42540688 · DOI: 10.1016/j.isci.2026.116861

NAT10 inhibition or haploinsufficiency corrected nuclear envelope and nucleocytoplasmic transport defects in iPSC-derived MAPT-mutant human neurons, and extended lifespan in a Drosophila tauopathy model, identifying NAT10 as a candidate therapeutic target. Senior author: Frederick J. Livesey.

Rationale: FTD-causing MAPT mutation studied directly in iPSC-derived human neurons — relevant to your iPSC platform interest.

14. The UNC13A cryptic exon associates with cognitive impairment in Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's Research & Therapy, 2026 · PMID: 42596024 · DOI: 10.1186/s13195-026-02157-7

In 1,672 AD brains, UNC13A cryptic-exon RNA levels (a TDP-43 cryptic-splicing target shared with ALS/FTD) predicted cognitive decline better than the UNC13A risk SNP itself. Senior author: Mercedes Prudencio.

Rationale: extends TDP-43 cryptic-splicing biology (an FTD/ALS mechanism) into AD cognitive decline — a mechanistically important cross-disease link. (Also appeared in Thursday's FTD/progranulin digest.)

15. Plasma p-tau217 and its association with frailty and renal function in hospitalized older adults

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42625502 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71768

In two Norwegian cohorts of acutely hospitalized older adults, plasma p-tau217 was not associated with frailty, but estimated glomerular filtration rate explained 27% of its variance; neurofilament light was the only biomarker significantly associated with frailty. Senior author: Mona Dixon Gundersen.

Rationale: top-tier AD journal, clinically important caveat for real-world p-tau217 interpretation — renal function needs to be accounted for in unselected/hospitalized populations.
