1. T-2 toxin disrupts Alzheimer's-relevant App/Tau homeostasis via circadian-p53-senescence crosstalk: Evidence from human neuroblastoma cells

Neurotoxicology, 2026 · PMID: 42680110 · DOI: 10.1016/j.neuro.2026.103559

In SH-SY5Y cells, low-dose T-2 mycotoxin exposure remodeled APP/Tau homeostasis (early APP loss, delayed sustained Tau phosphorylation) through a CLOCK-p53-senescence axis; pharmacological CLOCK or p53 inhibition attenuated Tau elevation and senescence, and senolytics (dasatinib/quercetin) reversed the phenotype (senior authors: Qinghua Wu and Kamil Kuca, co-corresponding).

Rationale: high relevance — direct mechanistic link between core clock protein CLOCK and Tau phosphorylation via a p53-senescence node, with a clean pharmacologic dissection and a senolytic rescue; a plausible circadian-senescence-tauopathy axis worth tracking, though the model (environmental toxin, immortalized cell line) is far from disease-relevant neurons.

2. Aging ADNP syndrome mice exhibit mutation/sex-dependent disruption of motor behavior and circadian rhythmicity

Neurobiology of Disease, 2026 · PMID: 42361864 · DOI: 10.1016/j.nbd.2026.107509

Cosinor-based rhythmometry in aged Adnp-mutant mice (haploinsufficient vs. p.Tyr718* tauopathy alleles) found strongly sex-dependent circadian disruption: males lost overall activity/rhythmic output, females showed phase shifts and fragmented waveforms, and Tyr718* males were markedly vulnerable to circadian-challenge-induced arrhythmicity (senior author: Illana Gozes).

Rationale: relevant mouse-model tauopathy work — ties a tau-associated genetic lesion (ADNP) to sex-dimorphic circadian phenotypes in aged animals, directly germane to mouse-model circadian/tau readouts and to sex as a biological variable in AD-adjacent disease.

3. Single cell transcriptomic analysis by deep learning identifies novel microglial transcription regulations in Alzheimer's Disease

AI in Neuroscience, 2026 · PMID: 42699651 · DOI: 10.1177/2997979x261472562

A deep-learning (MLP + SHAP) framework applied to ROSMAP microglial single-cell RNA-seq data classified AD vs. control nuclei, modeled disease-progression trajectories, and built transcription factor/regulon networks implicating dysregulated proteostasis, ER stress, and circadian rhythm pathways in synaptic and neuronal survival deficits.

Rationale: moderate relevance — microglial subcluster focus is adjacent to TREM2 biology and the ROSMAP dataset is high-quality, but circadian rhythm is only one of several downstream pathways flagged by the model, not a primary mechanistic finding; a niche journal.

4. Sleep quality is associated with default mode and salience network connectivity differently across age and sex

Neurobiology of Aging, 2026 · PMID: 42128467 · DOI: 10.1016/j.neurobiolaging.2026.05.002

Across two human cohorts (N=95 and N=1,244), poorer subjective sleep quality was associated with default-mode network hyperconnectivity and worse episodic memory in older women (a pattern resembling preclinical AD), while salience-network sleep associations shifted from hyperarousal-like in younger adults to a distinct pattern in older adults consistent with circadian/homeostatic decline (senior author: Ian M. McDonough).

Rationale: human imaging data linking sleep/circadian-adjacent decline to AD-relevant network signatures and episodic memory, with a sex-stratified effect; solid field journal, incremental but clinically grounded finding.

5. Physical inactivity in preclinical Alzheimer's disease: Risk factor, early symptom, or both?

Alzheimer's & Dementia (N Y), 2026 · PMID: 42703295 · DOI: 10.1002/trc2.70316

This perspective piece proposes that early tau accumulation in locus coeruleus/ventral tegmental catecholaminergic nuclei drives prodromal apathy and declining activity years before cognitive impairment, and argues for multidimensional digital phenotyping (incorporating circadian rhythms and life-space mobility) to distinguish "behavioral" from "biologically constrained" inactivity (senior author: Zhanjun Zhang).

Rationale: conceptual/perspective rather than primary data, and circadian rhythms are only one of several proposed digital-phenotype domains — included for its direct tie to early tau pathology in catecholaminergic nuclei and trial-enrichment implications, not for new experimental findings.
