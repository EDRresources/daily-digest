1. Postmortem pathologic burden predicts rate of clinical progression in frontotemporal lobar degeneration

Neurology, 2026 · PMID: 42479989 · DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000218278

Extending prior sequential-staging work on TDP-43 and tau in behavioral-variant FTD, this autopsy cohort links postmortem regional pathologic burden to the rate of antemortem clinical progression across FTLD. Senior author: David J. Irwin.

Rationale: top-tier journal, builds a Braak-like staging/progression framework for FTLD comparable to established AD neuropathologic staging — useful for trial-readiness and biomarker-to-pathology correlation.

2. C9orf72-associated and sporadic FTD patient iPSC-microglia differ in phagocytosis and gene expression

Stem Cell Reports, 2026 · PMID: 42561943 · DOI: 10.1016/j.stemcr.2026.103026

iPSC-derived microglia from C9orf72-HRE and sporadic FTD patients (roughly half of FTD cases lack a known genetic cause) show distinct phagocytic capacity and transcriptional profiles, suggesting genotype-specific microglial dysfunction rather than a unified FTD microglial signature. Senior author: Annakaisa Haapasalo.

Rationale: direct hit on iPSC-microglia modeling in FTD — a model system directly relevant to your translational platform, with a genotype-stratification angle worth tracking for TREM2/myeloid crosstalk.

3. RAN translation-coupled decay of the GGGGCC repeat transcript by the RNA exosome suppresses dipeptide-repeat production

International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2026 · PMID: 42589639 · DOI: 10.3390/ijms27156986

The RNA exosome surveils and degrades the C9orf72 GGGGCC hexanucleotide-repeat transcript in a manner coupled to repeat-associated non-AUG (RAN) translation, limiting production of toxic dipeptide-repeat proteins. Senior author: Tao Wang.

Rationale: identifies an RNA-decay checkpoint that normally restrains C9orf72 dipeptide-repeat toxicity — a candidate node for boosting endogenous clearance in C9orf72 FTD/ALS.

4. Clinical genome sequencing in neurodegenerative diseases — outcomes in the first 500 patients

Human Molecular Genetics, 2026 · PMID: 42561134 · DOI: 10.1093/hmg/ddag072

Rationale: real-world diagnostic-yield data from a leading FTD genetics group — useful benchmark for clinical genetic testing pathways, including in underserved populations.

Rationale: directly expands the GRN pathogenic-variant catalog with a structural-variant mechanism that standard sequencing panels can miss — relevant to your progranulin genetics interest.
