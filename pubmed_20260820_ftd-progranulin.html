1. Carboxyl-terminal blockade of sortilin binding enhances progranulin gene therapy in a mouse model of frontotemporal dementia

Science Translational Medicine, 2026 · PMID: 42616842 · DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.adr9158

In progranulin-deficient mice, gene therapy vectors expressing sortilin-binding-blocked progranulin achieved higher, more widely distributed progranulin levels and more effectively reversed microgliosis, lipofuscinosis, ganglioside/BMP lipid abnormalities, behavioral deficits, and elevated plasma neurofilament light than unblocked progranulin. Senior author: Erik D. Roberson (this is your own paper).

Rationale: top-tier journal, direct hit on your progranulin gene-therapy program — the sortilin-blockade cargo modification looks like a meaningful potency improvement ahead of/alongside ongoing clinical-stage GRN gene therapy efforts.

2. Splicing modulators reduce 4R tau and rescue tauopathy phenotypes in human neurons and in a mouse model

Science Translational Medicine, 2026 · PMID: 42616837 · DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.ady6759

Small-molecule splicing modulator compounds (SMCs) that promote MAPT exon 10 exclusion reduced 4R tau, hyperphosphorylated tau, and toxic tau proteoforms in FTD patient neurons carrying P301L or S305N mutations, and corrected the 3R/4R splice ratio and lowered pTau in vivo in an N279K gene-replacement mouse. Senior author: Elisabetta Morini.

Rationale: top-tier journal, field-relevant mechanism — pre-mRNA splicing modulation as a disease-modifying strategy for 4R tauopathies including MAPT-FTD, directly adjacent to your tau program.

3. Selective brain-penetrant TTBK1 inhibitors modulate TDP-43 pathology and rescue cognitive deficits in a mouse model of TDP-43 proteinopathy

Journal of Medicinal Chemistry, 2026 · PMID: 42593908 · DOI: 10.1021/acs.jmedchem.6c01882

Structure-guided medicinal chemistry yielded TTBK1-selective (over TTBK2, avoiding ciliogenesis liabilities), brain-penetrant inhibitors that reduced TDP-43 phosphorylation in cell and FTD patient-derived models, and in an FTD-TDP mouse model restored cognition, reduced microglial activation, and were neuroprotective in frontal cortex. Senior author: Ana Martinez.

Rationale: novel, selective small-molecule target validation for TDP-43 pathology in FTLD-TDP with in vivo cognitive rescue — a credible new therapeutic angle for the TDP-43 side of the FTD spectrum.

4. The UNC13A cryptic exon associates with cognitive impairment in Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's Research & Therapy, 2026 · PMID: 42596024 · DOI: 10.1186/s13195-026-02157-7

In 1,672 AD brains (plus UK Biobank replication), UNC13A cryptic-exon RNA levels in amygdala outperformed both the rs12973192 risk SNP and STMN2 cryptic splicing as a predictor of cognitive decline, despite no association with bulk TDP-43 pathology or survival. Senior author: Mercedes Prudencio.

Rationale: human genetics with functional/RNA-level validation, extending TDP-43 cryptic-splicing biology (shared with FTD/ALS) into AD cognitive decline — mechanistically important cross-disease link.

5. RAN translation-coupled decay of the GGGGCC repeat transcript by the RNA exosome suppresses dipeptide-repeat production

International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2026 · PMID: 42589639 · DOI: 10.3390/ijms27156986

The RNA exosome, via subunit EXOSC3, couples surveillance/decay of C9orf72 GGGGCC repeat-expansion transcripts to their own RAN translation, and EXOSC3 overexpression suppresses toxic dipeptide-repeat protein production in iPSC-derived neurons. Senior author: Tao Wang.

Rationale: identifies an endogenous RNA-decay checkpoint restraining C9orf72 dipeptide-repeat toxicity — a candidate node for boosting clearance therapeutically in C9orf72 FTD/ALS; note this paper also surfaced in an earlier week's digest via re-indexing, included here as it falls within this window's entry-date results.

6. Replicable generation and stable maintenance of rhesus macaque iPSCs for modeling of genetic frontotemporal dementia

Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology, 2026 · PMID: 42597777 · DOI: 10.3389/fcell.2026.1833030

Reports an ISSCR-compliant, reproducible pipeline for deriving and maintaining rhesus macaque iPSCs from a naturally occurring MAPT R406W FTD family, including optimized reprogramming and neural progenitor differentiation. Senior author: Jenna K. Schmidt.

Rationale: a genuinely novel model system — non-human primate iPSCs carrying a spontaneous MAPT mutation — directly relevant if you're weighing iPSC platforms beyond standard human lines for tau-FTD modeling.

7. Clinical tools to differentiate behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia from phenocopies

Alzheimer's & Dementia: Diagnosis, Assessment & Disease Monitoring, 2026 · PMID: 42614532 · DOI: 10.1002/dad2.70452

Prospective diagnostic-accuracy study in 51 participants found the FTDvsPPD checklist best discriminated bvFTD from psychiatric phenocopies (AUC 0.92, 80% sensitivity, 96.2% specificity), outperforming informant-reliant tools. Senior author: Peter J. Nestor.

Rationale: practical, well-validated bedside tool addressing a persistent bvFTD misdiagnosis problem; useful for clinic and for trial screening/enrollment accuracy.

8. Development and validation of a machine learning classification algorithm for differentiating frontotemporal dementia from Alzheimer's disease using automated brain MRI volumetry

American Journal of Neuroradiology, 2026 · PMID: 42498651 · DOI: 10.3174/ajnr.A9546

An XGBoost classifier using automated, asymmetry-aware brain volumetry distinguished FTD from AD and cognitively normal controls with 88.8% accuracy and AUC 0.93 on external validation (NIFD/ADNI training, tertiary-hospital validation), and reduced reader interpretation time when used as an assist tool.

Rationale: solid diagnostic-imaging tool with external validation and a reader study; incremental but practically useful for the AD-vs-FTD differential.
