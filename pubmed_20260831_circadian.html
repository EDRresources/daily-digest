1. Association of Sleep Variables With Cognitive Performance and the Risk of Dementia in the Sleep and Dementia Consortium

Neurology, 2026 · PMID: 42525904 · DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000218182

In a pooled analysis of 5 US cohorts (the Sleep and Dementia Consortium, n=5,958 for cognition, n=5,471 for dementia incidence) with home polysomnography and up to 19 years of follow-up, a data-driven clustering of 44 sleep metrics into 9 composites found that greater sleep quantity/efficiency and stronger slow oscillation-spindle coupling were associated with better global cognition, but none of the 9 composites predicted incident dementia (senior author: Jayandra Jung Himali).

Rationale: top-tier neurology journal, large multi-cohort dataset with objective polysomnography rather than self-report; the null result for dementia incidence despite cognitive associations is a notable, field-relevant negative finding on sleep as a dementia risk marker, though not directly a circadian-mechanism study and not linked to tau/progranulin/TREM2 biology.
