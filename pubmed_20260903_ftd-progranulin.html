1. Human TDP-43 expression worsens FTD-related phenotypes in progranulin-insufficient mice

Neurobiology of Disease, 2026 · PMID: 42264399 · DOI: 10.1016/j.nbd.2026.107475

Crossing progranulin-insufficient (Grn) mice with a human TDP-43 transgenic line showed that progranulin insufficiency did not induce TDP-43 aggregation but interacted with hTDP-43 expression to worsen social-dominance deficits, alter medial prefrontal cortex dendritic spines and RNA splicing, and drive a disease-associated microglial/impaired adaptive-immune signature. Senior author: Andrew E. Arrant (UAB Killion Center for Neurodegeneration and Experimental Therapeutics).

Rationale: work from your own center directly dissecting the GRN-TDP-43 interaction in FTD-GRN, implicating mPFC neuron dysfunction and dysregulated neuroinflammation as disease drivers.

2. Genetic frontotemporal degeneration across the lifespan? A critical appraisal of the neurodevelopmental hypothesis

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42659539 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71754

This review critically appraises preclinical and human evidence for a neurodevelopmental component to genetic FTD across the three major pathogenic variant groups (C9orf72, GRN, MAPT), concluding that no causal evidence yet supports the hypothesis despite converging correlational findings, and proposing frameworks linking early-life and neurodegenerative processes. Senior author: Elizabeth C. Finger.

Rationale: top-tier journal, field-shifting perspective directly spanning GRN and MAPT biology that could reframe how presymptomatic carriers are studied and when intervention windows open.

3. Engineered autophagy receptors administered with extracellular vesicles eliminate pathological Tau and TDP-43

Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2026 · PMID: 42649384 · DOI: 10.1038/s41551-026-01774-9

Fusing LC3A to cytoplasm-stable antibodies targeting Tau or TDP-43, then delivering these engineered autophagy receptors via small extracellular vesicles, reduced pathology in P301S tau primary neurons, TDP-43 iPSC-derived motor neurons, and after intravenous or AAV-mediated delivery in vivo. Senior author: Derrick Gibbings.

Rationale: top-tier journal, a genuinely novel targeted-degradation therapeutic modality addressing both tau and TDP-43 proteinopathy with demonstrated in vivo clearance and BBB-crossing delivery.

4. The PINK1-VCP-p47 pathway protects against tau-induced dendritic shrinkage in cortical neurons through interaction with STIP1

Neurobiology of Disease, 2026 · PMID: 42667935 · DOI: 10.1016/j.nbd.2026.107592

In mouse primary cortical neurons expressing 2N4R-Tau, PINK1, VCP, or phosphomimetic p47 fully rescued tau-induced dendritic shortening/simplification via enhanced STIP1 interaction and Rho-GTPase pathway engagement, while non-phosphorylatable p47 did not. Senior author: Charleen T. Chu.

Rationale: defines a specific, druggable PINK1-VCP-p47-STIP1 neuroprotective axis against tau-driven dendritic injury, complementary to mitophagy/proteostasis angles relevant to your tau program.

5. Robust tauopathy and memory deficits in a mouse model constitutively overexpressing human P301L MAPT

Neurobiology of Disease, 2026 · PMID: 42322996 · DOI: 10.1016/j.nbd.2026.107496

A new constitutive "cTau" line expressing 0N4R P301L human tau under the CaMKII promoter (without the rTg4510 tet-off system) reproduces age-dependent tau accumulation, gliosis, and spatial memory deficits by 6 months, with fewer genetic complications and no motor-phenotype confound. Senior author: Jada Lewis.

Rationale: a simplified, lower-cost alternative to rTg4510 for modeling FTLD-tau, directly relevant as a candidate mouse platform for tau-directed therapeutic studies.

6. Detection of TDP-43 proteinopathies in brain and cerebrospinal fluid using seed amplification assay

Current Issues in Molecular Biology, 2026 · PMID: 42651822 · DOI: 10.3390/cimb48080824

A streamlined, non-immunoprecipitation TDP-43 RT-QuIC assay detected seeding activity in 70% of ALS-with-dementia, 50% of FTLD, and 40% of ALS-only CSF samples, with >99% specificity in controls, and correlated with neurofilament light chain levels. Senior author: Ichiro Yabe.

Rationale: a technically simplified, scalable TDP-43 seed amplification assay is a meaningful step toward a clinically deployable biomarker for FTLD-TDP, useful for trial-readiness and diagnostic biomarker discussions.

7. MicroRNA signature in plasma-derived extracellular vesicles in frontotemporal dementia

Genes, 2026 · PMID: 42650138 · DOI: 10.3390/genes17080945

Validation of a prior NGS screen identified miR-365a-3p and miR-212 as significantly down-regulated in FTD patients' plasma EVs and PBMCs, with predicted target genes implicated in cell-cycle and apoptotic pathways. Senior author: Stella Gagliardi.

Rationale: candidate blood-based miRNA biomarkers for FTD differential diagnosis; mid-tier journal and modest sample size, but mechanistically plausible and easy to follow up.

8. The prevalence of frontotemporal dementia: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Neurological Sciences, 2026 · PMID: 42684472 · DOI: 10.1007/s10072-026-09371-z

Pooling 21 studies (38.7 million individuals), this meta-analysis estimated global FTD prevalence at 30 per 100,000, with extreme heterogeneity (I²=100%) and prevalence varying by age range but not region or diagnostic-criteria era. Senior author: Ying Huang.

Rationale: benchmark epidemiologic estimate useful for framing disease burden, though heterogeneity and underrepresentation of non-Western/Deep-South-comparable populations limit direct applicability to disparities work.

9. Prevalence of epilepsy and seizures in frontotemporal dementia: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Seizure, 2026 · PMID: 42664548 · DOI: 10.1016/j.seizure.2026.08.014

Across 11 studies (3,182 individuals), pooled prevalence of epilepsy/seizures in FTD was 7% overall, varying by clinical variant (3% in bvFTD vs. 9% in primary progressive aphasia), with substantial heterogeneity and sparse controlled comparisons. Senior author: Sonia Maria Dozzi Brucki.

Rationale: clinically actionable prevalence estimate for a underrecognized FTD comorbidity, useful for patient management though methodological heterogeneity warrants caution.

10. Longitudinal functional network connectivity changes across the clinical stages of C9orf72 hexanucleotide repeat expansion carriers

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42671157 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71744

Longitudinal resting-state fMRI in asymptomatic, prodromal, and symptomatic C9orf72 carriers versus controls showed stage-specific functional connectivity changes, with connectivity and gray-matter decline tracking baseline neurofilament light chain and symptom severity. Senior author: Suzee E. Lee.

Rationale: top-tier journal, adds longitudinal (not just cross-sectional) network-connectivity staging data for presymptomatic-to-symptomatic C9orf72 FTD, relevant to biomarker-based trial staging.

11. The highest clinical trial failure rate for any disease in modern medicine: how both amyloid- and non-amyloid-targeting therapies failed to alter disease course in Alzheimer's disease

Free Neuropathology, 2026 · PMID: 42683436 · DOI: 10.17879/freeneuropathology-2026-9650

Analyzing 497 classifiable Phase 2/3 AD trials (2010–2026), this single-author analysis argues that repeated trial failures — across amyloid, tau, TREM2, GLP-1, and progranulin/sortilin-restoration mechanisms alike — reflect institutional and regulatory path-dependency rather than purely intractable biology, and highlights presymptomatic prevention trials as the one strategy not yet refuted by the trial record.
