1. Human Stem Cell-Derived Models of the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimmune System

International Journal of Molecular Sciences (review), 2026 · PMID: 42653368 · DOI: 10.3390/ijms27167360

This review surveys hiPSC-derived 2D, 3D organoid/assembloid, and chimera models of microglia and astrocytes used to study AD risk variants in APOE and TREM2, familial PSEN1/PSEN2/APP mutations, and Down syndrome-associated AD, including recent work on Aβ/tau clearance in these systems. Senior author: Noah R. Johnson.

Rationale: a review, but directly on the iPSC-microglia model platforms and TREM2/APOE genetics your lab is expanding into — useful as a current-state reference for model selection and known limitations.

2. The TREM2 R47H variant is associated with liver-plasma-brain axis dyshomeostasis in the 5xFAD mouse model of Alzheimer's disease

Neurobiology of Aging, 2026 · PMID: 42102578 · DOI: 10.1016/j.neurobiolaging.2026.04.007

Lipidomic/metabolomic profiling of liver, plasma, and brain in Trem2R47H x 5xFAD mice at 4 and 12 months shows early, tissue-crosstalk dyshomeostasis along a liver-plasma-brain axis, with sex-dependent triacylglyceride effects and elevated brain 5-methyltetrahydrofolic acid in R47H homozygotes. Senior author: Mark Mapstone.

Rationale: direct primary data on a bona fide AD risk variant in a standard amyloid model, extending TREM2 lipid-sensing biology beyond brain to a systemic liver-plasma-brain axis — relevant to peripheral biomarker interpretation.

3. Macrophage TREM2 is a triglyceride-rich lipoprotein receptor that undergoes shedding in prediabetic hepatic steatosis

JCI Insight, 2026 · PMID: 42658581 · DOI: 10.1172/jci.insight.202675

Using human cohorts and mouse models of prediabetes/hepatic steatosis, the authors show macrophage ADAM17-mediated TREM2 shedding drives elevated plasma sTREM2 and APOC3-rich triglyceride-rich lipoproteins, while preserving full-length TREM2 protects against dyslipidemia and atherosclerosis. Senior author: Karin E. Bornfeldt.

Rationale: top-tier mechanistic dissection of the ADAM17/TREM2 shedding pathway that generates plasma sTREM2 — directly relevant to interpreting sTREM2 as a CSF/plasma biomarker in your AD cohorts, even though the disease context here is metabolic.

4. Soluble TREM2 Drives Atherosclerosis via HSP90β-Dependent Myeloid Inflammation

Circulation Research, 2026 · PMID: 42522548 · DOI: 10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.125.327491 · accompanied by an editorial, PMID: 42658903

In ApoE-deficient mice and human carotid plaque, exogenous sTREM2 binds HSP90β to activate IKK/IκBα/NF-κB signaling, promoting monocyte recruitment and macrophage inflammatory activation; the small molecule 3-bromopyruvate disrupts this interaction and attenuates atherosclerosis. Senior author: Ling Mao.

Rationale: top-tier journal, establishes sTREM2 as an active signaling ligand rather than an inert shedding byproduct — a mechanistic finding worth tracking given how heavily the field leans on sTREM2 as a passive fluid biomarker in AD.

5. Uncovering the biological mechanisms of TREM2 with molecular simulations: A comprehensive review and perspective

Progress in Biophysics and Molecular Biology (review), 2026 · PMID: 42288287 · DOI: 10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2026.06.002

This review synthesizes molecular dynamics studies of TREM2 structure, showing how disease-associated missense variants perturb the CDR ligand-binding sites, transmembrane signaling motifs, and multimerization interfaces, and outlines how simulation-guided design could inform small-molecule/peptide TREM2 modulators. Senior author: Kayla G. Sprenger.

Rationale: niche journal, but the only paper this week offering an atomistic mechanistic framework for TREM2 variant effects — useful background for structure-based agonist/modulator design.

7. Integration of TSPO PET and fluid biomarkers to assess neuroinflammation in Parkinson's disease

European Journal of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging, 2026 · PMID: 42219425 · DOI: 10.1007/s00259-026-07971-7

In 90 participants (71 PD, 19 controls), [18F]FEPPA TSPO PET binding potential in the pallidum correlated with motor severity, PD dementia, and plasma sTREM2, GFAP, and NfL levels, particularly in advanced disease. Senior author: Chin-Hsien Lin.

Rationale: not AD, but a solid imaging-fluid biomarker integration design (TSPO PET plus plasma sTREM2/GFAP/NfL) that is directly transferable to your dementia biomarker panels.

8. Microglial immunometabolism in Alzheimer's disease: A stage-resolved trajectory from adaptive remodeling to the metabolic paradox

Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy (review), 2026 · PMID: 42570638 · DOI: 10.1016/j.biopha.2026.119841

Proposes a stage-dependent "metabolic paradox" framework for microglial immunometabolic decline in AD, linking TREM2/APOE-dependent lipid handling, mitochondrial/proteostatic collapse, and defective phagocytosis, and stratifies claims by evidentiary strength (model-specific vs. patient-level). Senior author: Junliang Jiang.
