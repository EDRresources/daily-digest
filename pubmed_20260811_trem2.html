1. Review: microglial immunometabolism and the TREM2/APOE lipid axis across a stage-resolved AD trajectory

Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy, 2026 · PMID: 42570638 · DOI: 10.1016/j.biopha.2026.119841

This review organizes microglial immunometabolic changes in AD as a stage-dependent trajectory from adaptive remodeling to a "metabolic paradox" of decompensation, with TREM2/APOE-dependent lipid handling as a central axis linking bioenergetic decline to defective phagocytosis and impaired synaptic pruning. Senior author: Junliang Jiang.

Rationale: most directly on-topic synthesis this week, explicitly stratifying claims by evidentiary strength (mouse vs. human) and framing TREM2/APOE lipid biology as a mechanism-based immunoprevention target — useful conceptual scaffold, though a review rather than new primary data.

2. GPR146 deficiency boosts microglial Aβ phagocytosis and TREM2 upregulation while preserving BBB markers

Molecular Neurobiology, 2026 · PMID: 42554945 · DOI: 10.1007/s12035-026-06100-7

In Gpr146-knockout mice given intracerebroventricular Aβ42 oligomers, loss of the cholesterol-regulating receptor GPR146 potentiated microglial Aβ phagocytosis, upregulated phagocytic receptors including TREM2, GPR34, P2Y6 and CR3, and preserved blood-brain-barrier markers (claudin-5, GLUT-1) while reducing Aβ-induced lipid droplet accumulation. Senior author: Shaobin Yang.

Rationale: new primary mouse data nominating a druggable cholesterol-metabolism node (GPR146) upstream of TREM2-linked phagocytic receptor induction — relevant model system and mechanism for your microglial lipid-biology interests, though GPR146 itself is a new actor in this space and will need independent replication.

3. Review: neuroinflammation as a "third pillar" in AD, linking microglia/astrocyte state, BBB dysfunction, and tau propagation

International Journal of Developmental Neuroscience, 2026 · PMID: 42560134 · DOI: 10.1002/jdn.70168

This review integrates microglial and astrocytic state heterogeneity, BBB dysfunction, and peripheral immune infiltration into a temporal disease-stage framework for AD neuroinflammation, explicitly linking these processes to tau pathology and its stereotyped spread, and evaluating TSPO-PET and plasma GFAP as staging biomarkers. Senior author: Shaunt Papelian (single-author review).

Rationale: relevant framing paper connecting TREM2/microglial biology to tau propagation and biomarker strategy, but a broad narrative review with no new data — includable mainly for the stage-appropriate intervention-window argument, which bears on trial design.

4. TREM2 limits acute Toxoplasma gondii infection via ERK-dependent lysosomal/phagocytic control in macrophages

PLoS Pathogens, 2026 · PMID: 42555664 · DOI: 10.1371/journal.ppat.1014462

Trem2-knockout mice had higher mortality and parasite burden after acute T. gondii infection; Trem2-null macrophages showed reduced LAMP1 upregulation and impaired phagocytic clearance linked to elevated ERK signaling, and pharmacologic ERK inhibition rescued lysosomal function and parasite control. Senior author: Melissa B. Lodoen.

Rationale: not AD-focused, but a clean non-CNS demonstration that TREM2 loss causes an ERK-dependent lysosomal/phagocytic defect — mechanistically the same TREM2-ERK-lysosome axis implicated in microglial Aβ/tau clearance failure, so it's a useful cross-tissue validation of pathway logic.

5. Review: TREM2's stage-specific dual role in atherosclerosis, including the AL002a TREM2 agonist

Journal of Central South University (Medical Sciences), 2026 · PMID: 42565567 · DOI: 10.11817/j.issn.1672-7347.2026.260045

This review details TREM2's bidirectional role in atherosclerotic plaques — pro-lesion via CD36-mediated cholesterol uptake early, protective via efferocytosis and reverse cholesterol transport later — and notes soluble TREM2 as a candidate cardiovascular biomarker, plus TREM2 agonists such as AL002a as a strategy to reprogram foam macrophages. Senior authors: Liyi Liao and Xinqun Hu.

Rationale: lower-tier regional journal and non-CNS focus, but directly relevant because AL002 (the same TREM2-agonist antibody class in AD trials) appears here in a different disease context — useful for tracking cross-indication safety/efficacy signal on TREM2 agonism as a drug class.

6. TREM2⁺ tumor-associated macrophages suppress antitumor immunity via AHR-IDO1-kynurenine signaling in thyroid cancer

Frontiers in Immunology, 2026 · PMID: 42553364 · DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2026.1826288

Single-cell multi-omic profiling of thyroid cancer identified an immunosuppressive TREM2⁺ tumor-associated macrophage population driving CD8 T-cell dysfunction through the AHR-IDO1-kynurenine axis; pharmacologic AHR/IDO1 inhibition reversed suppression and reduced tumor burden in mouse models. Senior author: Fei Le.

Rationale: non-CNS and lowest priority of the six, but a well-powered single-cell mechanistic study of TREM2⁺ macrophage immunosuppressive programming — tangential relevance as a parallel example of TREM2⁺ myeloid cells adopting an immunosuppressive/dysfunctional phenotype, a theme echoed in disease-associated microglia biology.
