1. Plasticity of human microglia and brain perivascular macrophages in aging and Alzheimer's disease

Nature Genetics, 2026 · PMID: 42581323 · DOI: 10.1038/s41588-026-02716-6

Profiling 832,505 human myeloid cells from the prefrontal cortex of 1,607 donors across the lifespan and AD neuropathology spectrum, this study delineates six microglial/perivascular macrophage subclasses and maps how their proportions and transcriptional states shift with aging and AD progression. Senior author: Panos Roussos.

Rationale: field-defining — the largest human myeloid single-cell atlas of aging/AD brain to date, in a top-tier journal, directly relevant to TREM2-dependent microglial state transitions and disease-associated microglia biology.

2. Brain perivascular macrophages regulate endothelial cell function via a cMAF-dependent transcriptional program in mouse and human

Cell, 2026 · PMID: 42600613 · DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2026.07.043

Using single-cell multi-omics and conditional knockouts, this study identifies cMAF as a master transcription factor for brain perivascular macrophages (a TREM2-expressing myeloid population) and shows it is required for their regulation of blood-brain-barrier endothelial function in both mouse and human tissue. Senior author: Marco Colonna.

Rationale: top-tier journal, from a leading TREM2/myeloid biology lab; establishes a new transcriptional regulator of perivascular macrophages with direct relevance to neurovascular unit dysfunction in AD.

3. Proteomic signatures of protected APOE ε4 carriers reveal causal pathways associated with delayed Alzheimer's disease onset

Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2026 · PMID: 42592812 · DOI: 10.1002/alz.71688

Using harmonized plasma proteomics from the Global Neurodegeneration Proteomics Consortium, this study identifies proteins associated with delayed clinical onset in cognitively unimpaired APOE ε4 carriers, nominating candidate causal pathways that protect against AD despite high genetic risk. Senior author: Michael D. Greicius.

Rationale: top AD-specific journal; human genetics with functional/biomarker validation identifying resilience mechanisms in a well-defined at-risk population, relevant to therapeutic-target and biomarker prioritization.

4. Single-cell analysis of Powassan virus-infected brains reveals age-dependent neuroinflammatory crosstalk and progressive Alzheimer's-like APP/Aβ accumulation

mBio, 2026 · PMID: 42446869 · DOI: 10.1128/mbio.01294-26

Rationale: novel mechanistic angle — viral encephalitis as an age-dependent driver of amyloidogenic pathology — offering an alternative mouse model for studying neuroinflammation-amyloid crosstalk, though not TREM2-specific.
